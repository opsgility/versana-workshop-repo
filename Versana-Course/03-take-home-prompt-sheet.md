# Versana — Take-Home Prompt Sheet

> Keep this open alongside Copilot. Copy a prompt → replace `[bracketed]` text with your details → paste into Copilot → iterate. The best library is the one you customize to your day-to-day work.

---

## The Framework

Every prompt should answer these four questions. Even silently as you write the prompt:

- **Goal** — What do I want?
- **Context** — What does Copilot need to know about my situation?
- **Sources** — What files, threads, or transcripts should it look at?
- **Expectations** — How do I want the output? (length, tone, format)

## Email Drafting (Outlook)

### Cold reach-out to a peer team
> Draft a short email to `[recipient role / team]` about `[topic]`. The context is `[1-sentence background]`. I want to `[next step or ask]`. Tone: collegial, not stiff. Under `[100]` words. Don't open with "I hope this finds you well."

### Post-meeting follow-up
> Draft a follow-up email after `[meeting type]` with `[team]`. Reference the attached `[transcript / meeting notes]`. Capture: (1) decisions made, (2) action items with owners and due dates, (3) open questions, (4) the next-step ask. Under `[150]` words. Professional tone, no exclamation points.

### Tactful escalation
> Draft an email to `[partner / vendor / cross-team contact]` about `[issue]`. Reference `[document or runbook]`. Acknowledge the situation without finger-pointing. Ask for a specific next step. Under `[120]` words. Respectful but direct.

### Reply to a long thread you haven't read
> Summarize this thread in 4 bullets. Then draft a reply that addresses the most recent two messages, acknowledges the question raised in `[message N]`, and proposes `[a specific next step — e.g., a 15-minute call]`. Tone: professional, not stiff. Under `[100]` words.

## Status Updates & Check-Ins (Outlook)

### Weekly status update
> I'm a `[role]` sending a weekly status update to `[recipient]`. Draft an email with three sections: (1) Wins from this week — `[N]` bullets, specific. (2) Risks or blockers — `[N]` bullets, with the ask if any. (3) Next week — `[N]` bullets. Tone: confident, not over-explained. Under `[200]` words. Use bold for section headers but no other formatting.

### Check-in that isn't "just checking in"
> Draft a check-in to `[contact / team]`. We last spoke `[time ago]`. Reference `[attached file or recent change]`. Lead with a specific insight or update, not a generic greeting. Offer one concrete next step. Under `[100]` words.

### Polite decline of a meeting
> Draft a polite reply declining a meeting invitation. The meeting is `[meeting type]` that I don't think I add value to. Acknowledge the work's importance, offer to be looped in async on `[specific outputs]`, and suggest the organizer share `[the summary / agenda]` so I can flag if I should join a specific session. Tone: respectful, not apologetic. Under `[80]` words.

## Meeting Prep (Teams)

### Pre-meeting prep sheet
> I have a meeting in `[N]` minutes about `[topic]` with `[attendees / roles]`. I haven't reviewed `[attached file]`. Build me a one-page prep sheet with: (1) what I should already know — bulleted background, (2) three discovery questions I should ask, (3) one likely objection or concern and how to respond, (4) one risk or watch-out I should be ready for.

### Customer business review prep
> Prep me for a quarterly business review with `[customer]`. Attach `[account history / past transcripts]`. Give me: (1) relationship highlights from the last quarter, (2) two expansion topics worth raising, (3) one risk signal to listen for, (4) a 30-second opening I can read at the start of the call.

### Cross-functional kickoff prep
> Prep me for a kickoff meeting on `[initiative]`. Attach `[PRD / spec / scope doc]`. Build a prep sheet with: (1) the proposal summary in 3 sentences, (2) three discovery questions to surface unstated assumptions, (3) two risks to name explicitly, (4) the decision I want by end of meeting.

## Meeting Summaries (Teams)

### Decisions / action items summary
> Summarize this meeting transcript. Pull out: (1) decisions made, (2) action items with owners and due dates, (3) open questions, (4) a 2-sentence overview. Format as a Teams channel post — short, scannable, no buried information.

### Re-format for a specific audience
> Re-format the summary above for `[audience — e.g., the customer success channel / the exec team / the engineering Slack]`. Put `[the most important thing for this audience]` at the top. Strip anything that audience doesn't need to act on.

## Internal Memos (Word)

### Decision memo
> Draft a one-page internal decision memo for `[recipient — exec team, leadership]` on `[topic]`. Use this structure: (1) Issue — 2 sentences, (2) Background — 3–4 sentences, (3) Options — 2 or 3, with one anticipated benefit and one trade-off each, (4) Recommendation — clear, with a brief rationale, (5) Decision sought — what specifically you want from the reader and by when. Tone: neutral, decision-oriented. Reference the attached `[document]`. Keep to one page.

### One-pager / brief
> Draft a one-page internal brief on `[topic]` for `[audience]`. Use this structure: (1) What's happening, (2) Why it matters, (3) What we're doing about it, (4) What I need from you. Tone: confident, no jargon. Under one page.

### Pre-read for a meeting
> Draft a pre-read I can send to `[attendees]` before `[meeting]`. Summarize the attached `[doc]` in 4 sections: (1) Where we are, (2) What we're deciding, (3) Two options we're weighing, (4) The recommendation. Under one page. Tone: written, not bullet-listy.

## Customer-Facing Release Notes (Word)

### Sprint summary → release notes
> Convert this list of sprint-completed items into customer-facing release notes. Structure: (1) Headline (one sentence), (2) What's new — 3 highlights with a one-sentence customer benefit each, (3) Improvements — bulleted, terse, (4) Fixes — bulleted, terse. Tone: confident but not marketing-y. Avoid adjectives like "exciting," "powerful," "robust."

### Re-frame as customer benefits
> The "What's new" highlights above are too feature-focused. Rewrite them as customer-benefit-focused — what the customer can now do that they couldn't before. Lead each bullet with the user outcome, not the technical change.

## Vendor / Data Analysis (Excel)

> **Setup required:** Open the file in Excel for the web. Data must be formatted as an Excel Table (`Insert → Table`). AutoSave on. File in OneDrive. Then open Copilot in the ribbon.

### Pattern surfacing
> Summarize the patterns in this `[data type — vendor list / customer list / financial data]`. Which `[entities]` have the highest `[metric]`? Which have the lowest? Give me a `[N]`-row summary table I can paste into Teams.

### Watch-outs and bright spots
> Identify watch-outs (`[high cost]` AND `[low quality]`) and bright spots (`[low cost]` AND `[high quality]`). Show me the name, category, key metrics, and contract status for each. Format as two separate tables.

### Renewal-priority action plan
> Build a renewal-priority list — only the `[items]` with `[contract status criterion]`. For each, recommend an action: renew, renegotiate, replace, or expand. One sentence per recommendation. Sorted by `[priority criterion]`.

## Deck Outlines (Word → PowerPoint)

### Outline first in Word
> Outline a `[N]`-slide internal deck for `[audience]` on `[topic]`. Each slide should have a title and `[3–5]` bullet points. Slide 1 = `[content]`, Slide 2 = `[content]`, ... Slide N = `[content]`. Each bullet under 12 words.

### Then generate the deck in PowerPoint
> Use **Copilot → Create presentation from outline** (or paste the outline into Copilot's chat in PowerPoint). After the deck generates, iterate:
> *"Replace the title slide background with a darker theme. Make slide `[N]` the most visually emphasized — that's the recommendation."*

## SharePoint Quick Reference

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Before you prompt | Confirm the file is in OneDrive or SharePoint. Open it once so it's on your MRU list. |
| When attaching to a prompt | Say "reference the attached `[file name]`" — Copilot pulls from MRU. |
| After you draft | Save outputs (memos, release notes, summaries) to SharePoint, not OneDrive Personal. |
| Team-wide habit | Stop sending email attachments. Send SharePoint links. Whole-team Copilot results compound when files are shared. |
| When Copilot can't find a file | Check three things: (1) file in OneDrive/SharePoint, (2) opened recently (MRU), (3) permissions you can read. Fix one and re-prompt. |

## Two Universal Shapes for When You're Stuck

When your prompt doesn't match anything above, fall back to one of these two shapes:

### "Translate this into that"
> Translate `[input format — bullet list / transcript / email thread / spec doc]` into `[output format — internal memo / customer-facing release note / Teams summary / one-pager]`. Use this structure: `[give it a structure]`. Tone: `[tone]`. Length: `[length]`.

### "Review and iterate"
> I have a `[doc / email / deck]` I'm not happy with. Read it and tell me three things to fix — be specific, not generic. Then suggest a rewrite of the weakest section.

These two shapes cover roughly 80% of one-off Copilot asks.

---

## Three Habits to Carry Forward

1. **Use the framework on every prompt.** Goal → Context → Sources → Expectations. Even silently.
2. **Iterate, don't perfect.** First prompt rough, second prompt better, third prompt sent.
3. **Move your working files to SharePoint.** Copilot is only as good as what it can see — and your team's Copilot benefits compound when files are shared, not attached.
